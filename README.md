# Bass v. TTUHSC — Litigation Candor Catalog

An accuracy audit of court filings in litigation between Kevin Bass and
the Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center. It catalogs specific,
checkable places where a filing did not represent the record accurately
— a misstatement of the pleadings, of the record, or of legal
authority.

The two cases audited:

- **Federal** — *Bass v. Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center*,
  No. 5:25-cv-00244-H-BV (N.D. Tex., Lubbock Division)
- **Texas state** — *Bass v. Texas Tech University Health Sciences
  Center*, No. DC-2025-CV-1817 (237th Dist. Ct., Lubbock County)

## The documents

- **[`CANDOR_CATALOG_PLAIN_LANGUAGE.md`](CANDOR_CATALOG_PLAIN_LANGUAGE.md)**
  — the primary document. A plain-language guide written at roughly a
  6th–8th-grade reading level (Flesch-Kincaid grade 6.2). Each entry
  states where the issue is, what happened, the exact words written,
  why it matters, and how it was checked.

- **[`CANDOR_CATALOG_DETAILED.md`](CANDOR_CATALOG_DETAILED.md)** — the
  companion document. The full legal version of every entry, with rule
  citations, counter-cites, and the red-team test in legal language.

- **[`case_law/CASE_LAW_APPENDIX.md`](case_law/CASE_LAW_APPENDIX.md)** —
  the case-law appendix. Every legal authority the catalog relies on or
  audits, with case name, court, year, and precedential status
  confirmed against a copy of the CourtListener opinion database, the
  holding the catalog relies on, and a link to the full opinion.
  `case_law/case_law_index.json` is the machine-readable form.

## Methodology

Each entry catalogs a specific, checkable gap between what a filing says
and what the record says.

- **Every entry was red-teamed.** Before an entry was kept, the
  strongest honest defense of the challenged wording was written out
  and tested against the primary source. Only entries that still failed
  that test are here.

- **The plaintiff's own filings are audited under the identical
  standard** (Part III). This is a transparency audit, not a one-sided
  accusation: the same checkable-accuracy standard is applied to both
  sides.

- **Entries that rely on sealed court exhibits** have those references
  screened or redacted; the published portions stand on the face of the
  public record.

- **Every cited authority is listed in the case-law appendix.** Case
  name, court, year, and precedential status are confirmed against an
  opinion database, and each entry links to the full opinion — so a
  reader can check the catalog's characterization of any case against
  the source.

## What this catalog does NOT establish

- **Not bad faith or intent.** A misstatement is consistent with
  carelessness, a drafting error, or a good-faith disagreement about
  what a source says. The catalog records the gap; it does not assign a
  state of mind.
- **Not the merits of the underlying lawsuits.** This is an accuracy
  audit of filings, not a verdict on who should win.
- **Not a disciplinary finding.** Whether any entry warrants
  professional-conduct review is a separate question for the bodies
  that decide such things.
