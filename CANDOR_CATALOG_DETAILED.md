## Said a court decision says something it does not

<a id="s-04-s-05-s-06"></a>

### 1. Cited three Texas Supreme Court cases for a rule none of them states (S-04/S-05/S-06)

- **Filing:** The State's response opposing Mr. Bass's motion for a new trial, 237th District Court, Lubbock County, No. DC-2025-CV-1817 (Apr. 14, 2026). Signed by Assistant Attorney General Scott D. Smith.
- **Where:** pages 3–4

**What the filing says:**

> There, the Court held that a party who has knowledge of facts that would prompt a reasonable inquiry cannot rely on Rule 306a to extend deadlines.

> Texas courts have consistently rejected Rule 306a relief in similar circumstances where the movant had information that should have prompted further inquiry. See, e.g., In re Lynd Co., … John v. Marshall Health Servs., Inc., …

> The Texas Supreme Court has emphasized that litigants have a duty to monitor their cases and to take reasonable steps to protect their interests. See In re Lynd Co., 195 S.W.3d at 685.

**What the record shows:**

The response opposed Mr. Bass's sworn motion under Tex. R. Civ. P. 306a(5) and his motion for new trial. Its argument (§ III.A–B) was that a party who has information that should prompt inquiry cannot rely on Rule 306a, and that litigants have a duty to monitor their cases. It attributes that rule to three Texas Supreme Court decisions.

- *Memorial Hospital of Galveston County v. Gillis*, 741 S.W.2d 364 (Tex. 1987) (per curiam). The movant never proved in the trial court that she lacked notice within twenty days, so the trial court had no power to reinstate her case. The opinion contains no inquiry, diligence or monitoring rule; the response says the Court "held" one.
- *In re Lynd Co.*, 195 S.W.3d 682 (Tex. 2006). The Court held that a trial court may imply the date a party received late notice, and conditionally granted mandamus for Lynd, the party seeking post-judgment relief. The words "monitor," "duty," "diligence" and "inquiry" do not appear in the opinion.
- *John v. Marshall Health Services, Inc.*, 58 S.W.3d 738 (Tex. 2001) (per curiam). The Court reversed a dismissal for untimeliness, holding that the Rule 306a(5) motion did not have to be filed within thirty days of learning of the judgment. It does not address inquiry or diligence.

The misstatements are in the sentences quoted above, which present the cases as rejecting relief for failure to inquire and as imposing a duty to monitor.

**Evidence:**

- [Response, p. 3](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-14-state-defendants-response-motion-for-new-trial.pdf#page=3): “There, the Court held that a party who has knowledge of facts that would prompt a reasonable inquiry cannot rely on Rule 306a to extend deadlines.”
- [Response, p. 4](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-14-state-defendants-response-motion-for-new-trial.pdf#page=4): “The Texas Supreme Court has emphasized that litigants have a duty to monitor their cases and to take reasonable steps to protect their interests. See In re Lynd Co., 195 S.W.3d at 685.”
- [Gillis](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1730626/): “There is nothing in the record to indicate that Gillis sustained her burden of proving to the trial court that she had no notice or knowledge of the order dismissing her cause within twenty days of its rendition.”
- [Lynd](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/894734/): “In this mandamus action, we consider whether a court may imply the date on which a complaining party received late notice of judgment from an order granting a motion to extend post-judgment deadlines”
- [John](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1773194/): “but not that the appeal was untimely perfected. Accordingly, we reverse the judgment of the court of appeals and remand the case to that court for consideration of the merits.”

### 2. Cited a Supreme Court case for the opposite of what it says (F-40/F-68/F-82)

- **Filing:** The State's response opposing the motion to disqualify its lawyer (Doc. 50), U.S. District Court, Northern District of Texas (Lubbock), No. 5:25-cv-00244 (Apr. 28, 2026). Signed by Assistant Attorney General Scott D. Smith.
- **Where:** pages 4–5 and 12

**What the filing says:**

> The Fifth Circuit has squarely rejected disqualification based on the “mere possibility of conflict,” requiring instead a real and present conflict that threatens the fairness or efficiency of the proceeding. Dresser, 972 F.2d at 543; see also Wheat v. United States, 486 U.S. 153, 164 (1988).

> The Fifth Circuit has rejected disqualification based on “speculative or potential conflicts,” emphasizing that courts must avoid disrupting representation based on contingencies that may never arise. Dresser, 972 F.2d at 543; Wheat, 486 U.S. at 164.

**What the record shows:**

The response cites *In re Dresser Industries, Inc.*, 972 F.2d 540, 543 (5th Cir. 1992), and *Wheat v. United States*, 486 U.S. 153, 164 (1988), twice for the proposition that disqualification requires an actual, present conflict and that potential conflicts do not suffice (pp. 4–5 and 12).

- *Wheat* holds the opposite on this point: trial courts have "substantial latitude" to refuse waivers of conflicts "not only in those rare cases where an actual conflict may be demonstrated before trial, but in the more common cases where a potential for conflict exists," and the presumption in favor of chosen counsel "may be overcome not only by a demonstration of actual conflict but by a showing of a serious potential for conflict."
- *Dresser* granted mandamus and directed the district court to disqualify a law firm that was suing its own concurrent client. The phrases "speculative," "mere possibility of conflict" and "speculative or potential conflicts" do not appear in it; its only use of "possibility" is in a list of factors that favor disqualification.

**Evidence:**

- [Response, p. 5](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=5): “real and present conflict that threatens the fairness or efficiency of the proceeding. Dresser, 972 F.2d at 543; see also Wheat v. United States, 486 U.S. 153, 164 (1988).”
- [Response, p. 12](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=12): “The Fifth Circuit has rejected disqualification based on “speculative or potential conflicts,””
- [Wheat](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112074/): “not only in those rare cases where an actual conflict may be demonstrated before trial, but in the more common cases where a potential for conflict exists”
- [Wheat](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112074/): “that presumption may be overcome not only by a demonstration of actual conflict but by a showing of a serious potential for conflict.”
- [Dresser](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/588818/): “We therefore grant the writ, directing the district judge to disqualify counsel.”

### 3. Put three phrases in quotation marks that the cited cases do not contain (F-83/F-67)

- **Filing:** The State's response opposing the motion to disqualify its lawyer (Doc. 50), U.S. District Court, Northern District of Texas (Lubbock), No. 5:25-cv-00244 (Apr. 28, 2026). Signed by Assistant Attorney General Scott D. Smith.
- **Where:** pages 4–6 and 12

**What the filing says:**

> disqualification is “a severe sanction” that must be used sparingly and only when necessary to preserve the integrity of the judicial process. American Airlines, 972 F.2d at 611; Horaist v. Doctor’s Hosp. of Opelousas, 255 F.3d 261, 266 (5th Cir. 2001).

> the movant must demonstrate that the alleged conflict “clearly” threatens the fair and efficient administration of justice. Id.; Horaist, 255 F.3d at 266

**What the record shows:**

- "a severe sanction" (p. 4): attributed to *In re American Airlines, Inc.*, 972 F.2d 605, 611 (5th Cir. 1992), and *Horaist v. Doctor's Hospital of Opelousas*, 255 F.3d 261, 266 (5th Cir. 2001). Neither opinion contains "severe," "sanction" or "drastic."
- "clearly" (p. 6): attributed to "Id." (*American Airlines*, cited in the sentence before) and *Horaist*. In *American Airlines* the relevant use of the word is in the court's summary of Northwest's argument, which quoted comment 17 to Texas Disciplinary Rule 1.06. (The opinion's only other use of "clearly" is unrelated.) *Horaist* does not contain the word. The same sentence of the response also cites, with "see also," *Pistikopoulos v. Texas A&M University* (Tex. App. 2017) on a non-client's standing to challenge another party's lawyer. That case does not contain the word "clearly" either.
- "mere possibility of conflict" (p. 4) and "speculative or potential conflicts" (p. 12): attributed to *Dresser*, 972 F.2d at 543, and *Wheat*, 486 U.S. at 164. Neither opinion contains either phrase.

**Evidence:**

- [Response, p. 4](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=4): “disqualification is “a severe sanction” that must be used sparingly”
- [Response, p. 4](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=4): “The Fifth Circuit has squarely rejected disqualification based on the “mere possibility of conflict,””
- [Response, p. 6](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=6): “the movant must demonstrate that the alleged conflict “clearly” threatens the fair and efficient administration of justice. Id.; Horaist, 255 F.3d at 266”
- [American Airlines](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/588825/): “Comment 17 provides that alleged conflicts should be raised by an opposing party only where the alleged “conflict is such as clearly to call in question the fair or efficient administration of justice.””
- [American Airlines](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/588825/): “We reject both of Northwest’s arguments.”
- [Rule 1.06, comment 17](https://www.law.uh.edu/libraries/ethics/TRPC/1.06.html): “Where the conflict is such as clearly to call in question the fair or efficient administration of justice, opposing counsel may properly raise the question.”

### 8. Cited a page of a Texas Supreme Court decision for a rule that is not on that page (State-18)

- **Filing:** The State's Plea to the Jurisdiction, 237th District Court, Lubbock County, No. DC-2025-CV-1817 (Feb. 9, 2026). Signed by Assistant Attorney General Scott D. Smith.

### 12. Cited a subsection of a state regulation that does not exist (F-74)

- **Filing:** The State's response opposing the motion to disqualify its lawyer (Doc. 50), U.S. District Court, Northern District of Texas (Lubbock), No. 5:25-cv-00244 (Apr. 28, 2026). Signed by Assistant Attorney General Scott D. Smith.
- **Where:** page 13

**What the filing says:**

> Those definitions merely distinguish between retained counsel and full-time government employees for administrative purposes. See, e.g., 1 Tex. Admin. Code § 57.1(12).

**What the record shows:**

Rule 57.1 (Office of the Attorney General, outside counsel contracts) defines ten terms, numbered (1) through (10). Subsection (6) defines "Outside Counsel" as "[a]n attorney or law firm selected by an Agency to provide legal services," excluding full-time employees of the agency or the Attorney General's office. The text was checked against the current published version of the rule.

**Evidence:**

- [Response, p. 13](https://case.kevinnbass.com/assets/pdfs/filings/2026-04-28-dkt50-defendants-response-motion-to-disqualify.pdf#page=13): “See, e.g., 1 Tex. Admin. Code § 57.1(12).”
- [1 Tex. Admin. Code § 57.1(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/1-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-57-1): “Outside Counsel--An attorney or law firm selected by an Agency to provide legal services. The term does not include a full-time employee of the Agency or the Office of the Attorney General.”
